ctrl-surf / ctrl-prot are the mean-matched CONTROLS: the trainer multiplies
the TV edge weights by the phase factor without renormalizing, so a knob
that shifts the factor's mean is partly just a global tv_mult change (only
the NLM term sees a mean-1 normalized copy).  The controls replace the map
with a spatially constant factor of the same mean: tv_mult 11.292 for
surf1.0 and 7.905 for prot0.6.

Verdict: both knobs stay OFF by default.
 * phase_protect (det map): matched or beaten by the plain tv_mult control
   on every metric -- EPI(HV) 0.6622 vs 0.6660 and ENLr(HV) 0.4669 vs
   0.5090 favour the control -- so the map adds nothing beyond a scalar
   reduction of smoothing.  Consistent with the diagnostic
   (metrics_phase_maps_diag.txt): 'det' is near estimator noise.
 * phase_surface_boost (HH-VV map): effects are small (ENL-ROI +1.7%/+2.4%
   at s=1.0) and are reproduced by the mean-matched control; decisively,
   in the high-'surface' ROIs where the knob is supposed to act it shows NO
   advantage over the control (ENLsurf 1.1376 vs 1.1393).  Its only real
   edge is ratio-ENL(HV) (0.4281 vs 0.3848), and plain 'final' beats both
   there (0.4390) -- extra smoothing is simply not wanted on this patch.
 * The surface map IS a valid scene descriptor even so: ENL in its high
   ROIs is ~1.9x that in its low ROIs (1.10 vs 0.58) for every variant.
 * Side observation (not acted on): ctrl-prot (tv_mult=7.9) gives the best
   ratio-ENL(HV) of the whole table, 0.5090 vs 0.4390 at tv_mult=10, while
   ENL-ROI prefers the stronger smoothing -- the two criteria disagree
   about the tv_mult operating point.  Worth a dedicated sweep.
